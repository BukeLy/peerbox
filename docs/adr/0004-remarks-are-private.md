# What you call someone is your remark, not their alias

A remark (备注) is the name you give a person for your own use, such as 孔哥. Only you and your agents see it. You can give one to anyone on the instance, including group members who are not your contacts. Each person gets at most one remark from you, and none of your remarks names two people, so an agent that looks up a remark finds exactly one person. Removing a contact keeps the remark, because adding them again undoes the removal. An agent saves a remark only after its person has confirmed it.

## Considered Options

- Self-declared aliases, the first design: each person listed the names their contacts might call them, `whoami` told agents to collect that list, and lookups by strangers matched it across the whole instance. It was dropped for three reasons. What you call someone is your view of them, not theirs. Nobody can list in advance every name that colleagues, family and friends use for them. And because the list was public, any stranger could look up who goes by 孔哥, and anyone could claim 孔哥 for themselves.

## Consequences

A person's own chosen name is their display name (昵称), the way WeChat separates 昵称 from 备注. The first time a new contact comes up by a name the agent has not seen, the agent has to ask its person who is meant.
